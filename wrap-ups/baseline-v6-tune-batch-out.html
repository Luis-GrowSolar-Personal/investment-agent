0. Terms used in this report

In plain English first: this run had the analyst read 1,207 real earnings-call transcripts from 53 of the 54 companies in the “tune” group — a different set of companies than the ones already scored in the prior “train” run — and checked its recommendations against what actually happened to each stock. Two companies (Wolfspeed/WOLF and the current SunPower/SPWR) turned out to be cases where the ticker was reused by a different, reorganized company after a 2024–2025 bankruptcy, so the price history needed to grade their older calls no longer exists anywhere fetchable. On the other 51 companies, the analyst did slightly better than chance — and unlike the train result, the margin here is wide enough that luck alone can’t explain it.

train / tune / holdout
the three-way split of the 164-company corpus. Train was scored first and is the ruler every future candidate compares against. Tune (this run) is for prompt iteration. Holdout stays locked until a final promotion decision.
pp vs %
a percentage-point (pp) figure is an arithmetic difference between two percentages — not a relative change.
gradable
a scored call becomes gradable once its ticker has price coverage spanning both the call date and ~6 months later, and that forward window already exists in the frozen price data.
per_call_rec
the analyst’s own call (Hold / Add / Trim / Exit), not any downstream allocator or trend-layer action. Only this field is graded — the analyst/allocator firewall holds.
balanced accuracy
average hit-rate across the three possible answers, so “always guess the common answer” doesn’t score well by default. A skill-less guesser scores 33.3%.
luck-corrected gap
observed accuracy minus the accuracy you’d expect from the analyst’s own guessing habits. Zero means “no better than a weighted coin.”
95% range
a ticker-block bootstrap interval (resample tickers, not calls, with replacement; 5,000 resamples, seed 20201231). “Spans zero” means the data can’t rule out “no real effect.”
A6 grading
an existing project rule for companies whose equity was wiped out: grade the forward return as a maximal loss without needing a real forward price. Relevant here, but not currently wired into the scorer this run uses — see §Pre-checks.

On 51 of the 54 tune companies (1,168 gradable calls), v6 scores 28.3% against 25.7% expected by luck — a gap of +2.64 points, whose 95% range is 0.21 to 5.00pp. This is the first time in this project’s record that a luck-corrected gap has cleared zero. Balanced accuracy is 35.8% (95% range 32.7–38.7%), which still spans the 33.3% chance line.

Two of the 54 tune companies (WOLF, SPWR) could not be graded at all. Both are 2024/2025 corporate-reorg cases where the price history their pre-bankruptcy transcripts need has been fully purged from this project’s data source (confirmed live, not assumed — see Pre-checks). This is a 51-company test, with the exclusion disclosed and investigated, not the full 54-company test the prompt set out to run.

Carry the model confound in the same breath. Every figure here comes from claude-sonnet-4-6, not the retired model that produced v6’s original 40.4%/39.6% figures. That substitution’s one formal gate returned HOLD (7.4pp regression, 4.2pp noise floor) before the champion was retired anyway. Any weakness — or the positive gap above — may be the model, not the prompt, and this data can’t separate the two.

Which of the prompt’s three named outcomes is this? Closest to “holds and the range excludes zero” — but on tune’s own 51-company population, not yet on the pooled 107 companies together. Do not cite a pooled figure or a headroom-recomputation number from this run — that step belongs to the design session. What can be said now: the train result replicates in direction and magnitude, and tune’s own interval is tight enough, on its own, to exclude zero where train’s wasn’t.

Pre-checks — reported explicitly, per the prompt

Ticker aliases

Swept all 54 tune tickers against TICKER_ALIASES.json. Only GOOGL has an entry (→ working symbol GOOG). No other tune ticker is aliased.

WOLF price coverage

Investigated live, not guessed. A first probe showed WOLF trading today (~$24–29/share) — not permanently delisted, which looked like a simple backfill gap. A second, more careful probe (2019–06 through 2025–06) returned zero rows: Yahoo has purged WOLF’s pre-petition price series entirely, matching the same empty-series shape already documented for three other verified wipeouts (NOVA, SUNW, FRC). Consistent with Wolfspeed’s 2025 Chapter 11 — trading resumed 2025-09-29 under the same ticker, but economically a different security.

Decision: left WOLF’s calls in the fetch/scoring scope (a durable asset for later), but did not force a synthetic zero-price entry into the price cache — that would be new scorer behavior this prompt didn’t authorize. NOVA is already silently ungradable through this same scorer today, for the identical reason.

Coverage guard — and the finding it caught

Confirmed present and passing on the existing train eval cache before relying on it here (reproduced 30.3%/27.8%/+2.48pp/35.5% exactly). Grading the full 53-ticker tune population then failed the guard — not only for WOLF (100% loss, expected) but also for SPWR, which lost 10/15 calls (67%), unanticipated by the prompt.

Investigated: scorer_price_cache_v1.json’s SPWR series starts 2023–01–03, not the corpus window start. A live probe confirmed the identical purge pattern as WOLF: the original SunPower Corporation filed Chapter 11 in 2024, and the SPWR ticker was reused by a reorganized entity afterward. The price-cache build had marked SPWR "status": "ok" because the fetch itself succeeded — it never checked whether the series covers the corpus window.

Per the prompt’s explicit rule, the guard was not overridden. This report covers the 51 tune tickers unaffected by this bankruptcy-driven ticker-reuse pattern, with WOLF and SPWR’s exclusion stated up front — a disclosed scope restriction, not a silenced guard.

Findings

  • A bug the pilot caught before it touched the real batch spend. The driver’s transcript lister originally scanned the whole shared transcripts directory (train and tune coexist there) instead of filtering to tune’s own tickers. The first pilot run scored 5 ABBV transcripts — a train ticker — by mistake ($0.4321 wasted, results discarded, not written to any eval cache). Fixed before the batch submitted. Unfixed, the ~$47 batch would have re-scored up to 1,240 already-scored train calls, violating the “train split only” hard constraint and roughly doubling real spend.
  • BK/BNY: 0 transcripts. The vendor’s only in-window-adjacent event for BNY falls after the corpus window — BNY only began trading under that ticker in 2025 post-rebrand, and pre-rebrand BK history isn’t carried forward. 53/54 tune companies got transcripts.
  • Two explicit 404 coverage misses: GIS 2026Q1, SRE 2020Q1. 1,207 of 1,214 planned in-window transcripts landed.
  • Two transient network drops mid-fetch, both resumed cleanly with zero re-fetches (per-transcript flush-to-disk design).
  • Ground truth in the gradable sample skews bearish (48.4%) — the same pattern train showed on its own gradable slice.

Limitations, stated plainly

  • 51 of 54 tune companies graded, not the full split — WOLF and SPWR excluded for a specific, investigated, disclosed reason, not dropped silently.
  • The model confound stands: claude-sonnet-4-6 is not the model v6’s original figures were measured on.
  • The corpus over-represents failures by construction, carried over unchanged from the legacy framing.
  • Sector-relative grading is unresolved; everything is benchmarked against SPY.
  • The tune interval excluding zero is on its own, standalone 51-company population — not yet checked against train under any joint/pooled test, and not a promotion-grade result on its own.
